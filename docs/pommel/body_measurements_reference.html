Why the pommel is low over the 3 screws

The tall part of the pommel sits where the knees are, not where the thighs meet the body. The part over the screws is only a flat anchor plate. These sketches put a small and a big child on the chair with the real CAD outlines so you can see where everything lands.

Why not make it tall over the screws too?

  1. That spot is the groin. Seated against the backrest, the groin is about 80 mm (small child) to 125 mm (big child) from the backrest, which is right above the saddle and its screws. Seating guidance is clear that a pommel ("medial knee support") must sit clear of the groin, because the groin is not built to carry load. A tall block there presses on the genitals and pubic bone, causes pain and pressure marks, and gets dirty.
  2. It works at the knees, not at the hips. The thighs start together at the groin and spread toward the knees, so the gap between them is almost nothing near the body and largest at the knees (58–75 mm at a natural 5°). A spacer works best where the gap is, at the far end of the thigh: a small force there holds the knees apart. A block near the groin would only squeeze soft tissue.
  3. It must not be used to stop sliding. If the child slides forward, a tall post near the groin would catch them by the crotch. Sliding is the job of the pelvic belt, not the pommel. Keeping the area low makes that mistake impossible.
  4. Getting in and out. Parents lift the child in and out over the front of the seat. A post at the groin is in the way and is a knock point during transfers and on bumps.

So the design rule from the panel was: nothing taller than about 10 mm closer to the backrest than 145 mm (ages 2–3) or 170 mm (ages 4–5). The saddle follows it: it is 1.5 mm above the seat at the rear screw, about 5 mm at the front screw and 10 mm at the seat edge. On the long sizes the neck also stays low (1–6 mm above the seat line) under the thighs, and only rises into the pad where the knees are.

What this means for fitting

  • Small child: the knees are only about 50 mm past the seat edge, so the short S pommel's pad (0–75 mm past the edge) sits right at the knees.
  • Big child: the knees are about 170 mm past the edge. The long K45 pad covers 85–155 mm, so it holds the inside of the lower thigh just behind the knee. For a tall child, use the reach adjust (+15 or +30 mm) to bring the pad closer to the knee.
  • If a child needs more control higher up the thigh (strong adductor tone), the safe direction is a longer pad toward the seat edge, still stopping before the keep-low line, rather than a taller saddle. That is a PT decision.

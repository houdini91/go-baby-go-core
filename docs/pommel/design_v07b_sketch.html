GbG pommel v0.7b: saddle, 90° hook and triangle bracing

Replaces the slot-and-tongue sketch. Dimensions in mm, chair coordinates. Blue = saddle, orange = pommel, black = carbon rod, green dashed = the load-carrying triangles, red = bolts / cuts into the chair.

Why this is stronger

  • Pressing down on the pommel pushes the hook against the chair's front face and the brace (triangle) carries it there in compression. Plastic is strong in compression; nothing bends out of a slot.
  • The 3 existing M3 bolts are only pulled straight up (estimated 100-200 N each, against well over 2,000 N capacity), into the existing nut pockets under the seat.
  • Pushing sideways: the saddle is a triangle in plan, 80 mm wide where it hooks the seat edge, so the twist is spread across the whole hook (and the shallow recess walls, if added), not three bolts in a line.
  • Groin: the saddle is only 3-4 mm tall at the rear bolt (nearly flush in the recess) and gets thick only at the seat edge, ~177 mm from the backrest.

Short pommel (one printed part, no rod)

Long pommel (same saddle + 2 carbon rods)

  • The neck and the rods form a truss: the rods are the bottom chord, the printed neck the top. The rods also go 57 mm into the chair, so the chair carries the reach, as you wanted.

How it becomes one solid piece (nothing loose on the rods)

  • The pommel is one print: saddle, hook, neck and knee pad together (~215 mm long, fits the P1S bed). The rods are not a joint between parts; they are stiffeners inside one part.
  • Press fit: each rod channel has 3 small crush ribs. The rod is pushed in by hand (or tapped with a mallet) and the ribs deform to grip it with zero play. No glue, nothing to measure.
  • Locked: the neck has a slit through both rod channels and one M3 bolt (kit hardware) across it. Tighten it and the slit closes and clamps both rods, like a bike seat-post clamp. Pommel + rods are now one rigid piece.
  • Into the chair: the rod ends slide into the chair holes (slip fit) as the hook goes over the seat edge, then the 3 seat bolts go in. The rods are now trapped at both ends and can't move in or out.
  • Optional, for a permanent build: a drop of epoxy in the channels. Not needed with the clamp.

Chair change (optional, on a branch)

  • Shallow recess, 3 mm deep, on the seat top from the bolt row to the edge and down the front face, in the triangle shape. The saddle nests flush and the recess walls take the sideways twist.
  • 2× Ø6.2 rod holes, 57 deep, in the pan front face at x = ±12, z = 8 (clear of the M3 bolts and nut pockets). Only used by long pommels.
  • The 3 M3 holes and their nut pockets stay exactly as they are.
  • Without any chair change, the short pommel still fits today's chair (no recess: a little less twist resistance; test it).

Why I started with dots: they're the least work (peel and press, no cutting) and they line themselves up. Strips may hold the edges better. That's why both are in the test, on the same coupon. Adhesive matters more than the hook: choose "3M" or acrylic glue. Rubber glue lets go in a hot car (50–60 °C).

5. Test plan (on the coupon, then on the winning full shell)

  1. Heat forming: cut a piece of each foam about 70 × 110 mm (the wing area of the template). Form it over the ball or into the coupon (with baking paper, briefly). Pass: after 24 h it still follows the curve, with less than 2 mm of edge lift when laid in the coupon with no fixings.
  2. Hold: attach it with dots on one coupon and strips on another, or the sticker on the plain zone. Pull the edge by hand, and push sideways the way a head turns. Pass: nothing lifts and nothing slides.
  3. Re-attach: peel off and put back 10 times. Pass: the dots or strips stay stuck to the shell and the foam.
  4. Hot car: 1 h on a car dashboard in the sun, or in an oven at 60 °C with the door ajar. PETG is fine to about 70 °C. Pass: the adhesive doesn't creep or let go.
  5. Clean: wipe with soapy water and alcohol. Pass: no swelling, and the edges stay sealed.
  6. Feel: press with a thumb, and ideally the therapist judges. Firm vs soft decides the EVA sheet vs the play mat, or a 5 mm adhesive top layer.

Record the results per material and method, and we lock the winner into F3.

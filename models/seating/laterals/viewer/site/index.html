Round 5 direction: one small mount that completes the wing

A concept to review, not CAD yet. One mount only. Just a 40 mm piece of the wing's front edge belongs to the kit (the "nose"). It has the blade's own width and follows its edge, and the arm grows out of its front. Nothing sits above or below it, and the chair's old mount (its block, pocket and clamp-bolt holes) is gone too. Heights come from 2 arm lengths × the LOW/HIGH flip × 2 screw positions on the pad (table on the right), not from more mounts. The release button sits on the nose's outer side, square to its press, and the button is the catch: one sliding part, pressed straight in to free the arm.

The button: final

12 × 24 mm stadium on the nose's outer face, at the arm's height, on the side away from the child. The face is square to its press (no sideways slide), with a shallow thumb scoop. Chair colour, with an orange ring that shows only when it isn't latched. Flush means latched.

How it works: the button and the catch are one sliding part. Its tooth sits in a groove in the wing. To remove: squeeze the button with your thumb against your fingers on the inner face (5 mm travel to a hard stop), then pull the arm forward. To fit: push the arm in until it clicks.

Agreed upgrades: a bigger tooth, sized for the corner knock (about 900 N, from the latest load check); a 6° tapered locking face so it can't rattle; a sharp-edged entry for a clear click. A bought steel dowel as the tooth is a coupon-test option. To check: thumb room beside the car's side wall.

The spring doesn't need to be strong

The arm is held by the shape of the tooth in its groove, not by the spring. The plastic tooth takes the pull-out load (up to about 900 N in a hard corner knock, so it's sized for that). The spring only pushes the button back out.

Proposed: OD 6, wire 0.55, free length 16, 9 coils. That's about 4 N at rest and 8 N fully pressed, so the press is about 9 N. The comfortable thumb range is about 3–23 N. While a child leans on the pad, friction on the tooth stops it releasing: unload the pad, then press.

Building…

Accepted Four carriers per arm length: LOW and HIGH can't be one part turned over

The wing isn't vertical: going up, it leans about 3° outward and 4° backward. The nose copies that lean so it sits flush. If you turn a LOW carrier over to use it HIGH, the lean goes the wrong way. At the top and bottom of the nose it is then about 2.3 mm off sideways and 2.8 mm off front-to-back (red). It wouldn't go in, and the step would show. So LOW and HIGH are each made the right way up. The right side shows a correctly made HIGH carrier fitting.

Why accepted: a child's set still uses only 2 carriers (one per side). The cost is more print files (left/right × LOW/HIGH per arm length), not more parts per child.

The alternative was a symmetric nose that ignores the lean. One part would then flip, but the nose would show a step of about 2.5 mm against the wing at its top and bottom.

Building…

Accepted How the button goes in: dropped in from the top

The button can't be pushed in through its face, so it goes in from an opening in the top of the nose. Once the carrier is on the chair, the wing covers that opening. The spring goes onto the button, and both drop down the opening. Then the split orange sleeve snaps in under the rim and keeps the button captive. The carrier then slides onto the wing and clicks. To remove it: press and pull. Use Next / Play; the carrier is shown cut open.

Why accepted: a one-piece button can't go in from outside if the walls behind it are to carry load.

With the strength fixes: the latch moves out of the tongue into the nose, so the tongue stays solid and strong. The sleeve is split, so it can be prised out with a pick to service the button. A closed ring could only be cut out.

Building…

Accepted The pad screw goes in from the child side, under the foam

The pad slides onto the foot's dovetail from behind. A nut hides in the foot's groove. The M5 × 10 button-head screw goes in from the child side, and the foam and cover then go over its head. A small plastic snap stop on the rail (green) clicks into a notch in the groove. The plastic holds the pad, and the screw only locks it. Shown cut through the screw, long arm, LOW, lower pad hole (the Onshape fit).

Why accepted: with two pad positions, both nuts can hide inside the foot. Nothing shows on the back, and the screw head is under at least 10 mm of foam (the review's minimum). The unused groove's open back end shows on the foot's back face (cosmetic only).

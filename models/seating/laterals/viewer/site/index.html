Round 5 direction: one small mount that completes the wing

A concept to review, not CAD yet. One mount only. Just a 40 mm piece of the wing's front edge belongs to the kit (the "nose"). It has the blade's own width and follows its edge, and the arm grows out of its front. Nothing sits above or below it: the block, tall slot, rails and slot blanks all go. Heights come from 2 arm lengths × the LOW/HIGH flip × 2 screw positions on the pad (table on the right), not from more mounts. The release button sits on the nose's outer side, square to its press, and the button is the catch: one sliding part, pressed straight in to free the arm.

Building the draft…

The button: final

12 × 24 mm stadium on the nose's outer face, at the arm's height, on the side away from the child. The face is square to its press (no sideways slide), with a shallow thumb scoop. Chair colour, with an orange ring that shows only when it isn't latched. Flush means latched.

How it works: the button and the catch are one sliding part. Its tooth sits in a groove in the wing. To remove: squeeze the button with your thumb against your fingers on the inner face (5 mm travel to a hard stop), then pull the arm forward. To fit: push the arm in until it clicks.

Agreed upgrades: a bigger tooth, sized for the corner knock (about 900 N, from the latest load check); a 6° tapered locking face so it can't rattle; a sharp-edged entry for a clear click. A bought steel dowel as the tooth is a coupon-test option. To check: thumb room beside the car's side wall.

The spring doesn't need to be strong

The arm is held by the shape of the tooth in its groove, not by the spring. The plastic tooth takes the pull-out load (up to about 900 N in a hard corner knock, so it's sized for that). The spring only pushes the button back out.

Proposed: OD 6, wire 0.55, free length 16, 9 coils. That's about 4 N at rest and 8 N fully pressed, so the press is about 9 N. The comfortable thumb range is about 3–23 N. While a child leans on the pad, friction on the tooth stops it releasing: unload the pad, then press.

How the latch works: today vs. round 5 (cut from above, at the button)

Both catches do the same job: a small block (teal) sits in a groove in the wing's outer wall and stops the arm being pulled out. What changes is how your thumb moves it. Today you press the button along the arm, into the wing (orange arrow). A push-rod carries that motion back, and an angled slot turns it 90° to slide the catch sideways out of its groove. In the draft the button and the catch are one piece. You press sideways, toward the child, and the same piece slides straight out of the groove. Same motion, same direction, no conversion.

    1 · Wing slot and height

    The tongue (top of the carrier) slides down into the vertical slot in the wing tip. A 2.5 mm rail on the slot's inner wall, one per height position, runs in a groove along the tongue: that sets the height and stops it tipping.

    Slot blanks fill the unused slot above and below. Try Wing slot.

    2 · Latch

    The pill button is the end of a 5.6 mm square push-rod. Pressing moves the rod 3.5 mm into the wing; an angled slot in the rod pulls the shuttle's peg sideways 4.5 mm, out of the catch groove in the wing's outer wall. The spring behind the rod pushes it all back.

    Use Latch (it cuts along the rod), then drag Press.

    3 · Carbon rods

    An Ø4 and an Ø3 carbon rod are bonded into bores from the tongue's rear face. They run through the slot mouth and 18–20 mm into the arm, where the bending is highest.

    4 · Arm-to-depth joint

    A 13 × 20 mm tenon on the arm end slides 13 mm into the depth piece's sleeve; only a 0.3 mm hairline shows. Four Ø2 carbon pins cross the tenon root and run 8 mm into the arm. One M5×12 countersunk screw from the back face (+y) pulls into a nut inside the tenon.

    Use Arm-to-depth joint.

    5 · Pad mount

    The depth piece flares into a foot that sits on the pad back. A 20 mm rib on the pad back keys into a groove in the foot. One M5×10 button-head screw sits on the floor of the depth piece's hollow and pulls into a nut in a pocket on the child face of the pad (under the foam).

    6 · Cover

    The neoprene cover wraps the pad edge and is trapped in the groove in the pad back by the clip ring. No sewing. The navy body is the reference foam and cover, not printed.

    The release button: what's wrong, and five options

    Diagnosis (the designer and mechanical-engineer reviews agree). The button pushes straight into the wing, along the push-rod. But where the rod line comes out, the arm surface faces almost straight outboard, about 50° off that axis (38–61° across the cap). So:

    • The 7 × 9 pill, projected onto that slanted face, becomes an 11.3 × 9 lopsided egg, with an uneven gap and a 0.25 mm knife-edge lip at one end.
    • A thumb pushes square to the face, so it shoves the rod sideways about as hard as it pushes it in (side/push ≈ 1.1–1.5). With PETG friction (μ 0.3–0.5) that can wedge the rod: pressed at the end nearest the wing it self-locks from μ ≈ 0.51; mid-cap needs an estimated 38–99 N.
    • By the time it releases, the cap is about 2 mm below the surface, and release needs 3.4 of the 3.5 mm travel, so a thumb barely reaches it.

    A true 12 × 9.5 stadium drawn on the surface, even 0.3 gap, softened edges. Still flush, still sleek.

    2 · Clean pill + 3 grip lines

    As 1, with three fine grooves (0.7 wide, 0.4 deep, 2.2 pitch) cut across the flow.

    The grooves give the thumb purchase to push along the rod (≈ 14–17 N) instead of square to the face.

    3 · Angled landing (designer's pick)

    A small 12 × 15 flat facet turned 20° toward the push. A flat 8 × 11 cap sits flush in it with a 2 mm ledge all round: your "ledge" idea.

    Off-axis drops from ~50° to ~28–35°. Self-lock would need μ ≥ 1.1 (not possible); about 23 N to press (18 N lubed). The cap prints face-down, crisp.

    4 · Thumb scoop

    A soft teardrop dish (3–3.5 mm deep) with an 8 × 10 cap in its floor and a small dimple to find by feel. Nothing stands proud.

    Guides the push well, but removes the most material where the arm is already near its strength limit.

    5 · Small dome

    A true 11 × 8 pill with a 1 mm crown: the "small bulge".

    Easy to find blind, but the push angle is unchanged and it sticks out unevenly on the slanted face. Toddlers find bumps.

    Fit fixes for whichever option you pick (no visible change)

    • Rod clearance 0.2 → 0.25 mm per side, a 0.5 mm chamfer on the rod's rear end, and a 0.3–0.4 mm edge break on the cap and pocket rim (removes the knife lips).
    • A 45° peaked roof on the rod channel, so it prints without a sagging bridge when the carrier lies on its outer face.
    • Print the button standing on its rod end. Dry PTFE lube on the rod.
    • Coupon test: print the current cap and the chosen one, and measure the release force pushing square to the face vs. along the rod.

    Recommendation: 3 (angled landing) + fit fixes is the only option that fixes the look, the jam and the reach together. 2 (clean pill + grip lines) is the conservative, fully flush alternative if you'd rather have no facet. Ruled out: tilting the rod to meet the surface square (it would collide with the rail groove) and sliding the button along the rod line (every exit point there is ≥ 30° off).
